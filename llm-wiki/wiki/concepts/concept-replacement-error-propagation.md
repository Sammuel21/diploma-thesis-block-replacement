# Replacement Error Propagation

## Overview

A replacement operator can approximate its original target well on cached
inputs while still degrading the complete model. Once inserted, its output
error becomes part of the input to downstream layers. Replacing several
operators compounds this distribution shift. Grafting identifies this as the
reason local initialization alone is insufficient. [src-grafting-2025,
Sections 1 and 3.1]

## Definition or Description

For original operators `f_i` and substitutes `g_i`, local training observes
inputs generated by the original upstream computation. During integrated
inference, a later substitute receives states produced by earlier substitutes:

```text
original:     x_i = f_(i-1)(...f_1(x_0))
replacement: x'_i = g_(i-1)(...g_1(x_0))
```

Even when `g_i(x_i)` is close to `f_i(x_i)` on cached calibration states,
behavior on shifted states `x'_i` may differ. The final error therefore depends
on interaction and composition, not only the sum of isolated training losses.

## Evidence and Rationale

Grafting's self-grafting experiment replaces all operators with identical
randomly initialized architectures. Local activation regression improves each
operator, but model-level fine-tuning is still required to recover near-baseline
quality. Increasing Stage 2 data improves the integrated result.
[src-grafting-2025, Section 3.3; Table 2]

**Synthesis.** This creates two distinct evaluation levels for the thesis:
block-level approximation error measures isolated functional matching, while
model-level loss and perplexity measure the composed system after replacement
and any recovery.

## Limitations and Open Issues

The current evidence is from diffusion transformers. The magnitude and shape
of propagation in causal LLMs may depend on residual connections, normalization
placement, replacement depth, token distribution, and the number and spacing
of replacements.

Local MSE cannot be assumed to predict model-level degradation monotonically.
The thesis should test that relationship directly and report both levels.
