# Thesis results, snapshot 2026-10-15

Homogeneous SwiGLU replacement against MLP weight quantization on pinned
SmolLM2-1.7B. Both methods change only the MLPs of layers 1-22. Metrics follow
[core-eval](../methodology/core-eval.md); details are in
[SwiGLU-8](../experiments/model/swiglu/swiglu-8.md),
[Quantization-1](../experiments/model/baseline/quantization-1.md), and
[Speed-1](../experiments/model/baseline/speed-1.md).

## Headline

| Question | Answer so far |
| --- | --- |
| Size: who keeps more quality at equal bytes? | Quantization, at every budget it reaches (up to 47.5% saving) |
| Compute: what does SwiGLU buy? | Up to 52% fewer FLOPs per token in the weight matrices; quantization removes none |
| Speed: is SwiGLU faster? | Faster to first token (16% at 50%, 25% at 80%), same generation speed; quantization is never faster |
| Does SwiGLU's local fit matter after recovery? | Yes at 100M (10-24% lower KL), but the gap is still shrinking |

## 1. Size: quality at equal bytes

Byte saving is for the whole model. SwiGLU-8 rows are the fitted arm after
100M recovery tokens. A variant is on the frontier when nothing else saves at
least as many bytes with lower 8K KL.

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Dense BF16 | 0.0% | 3.19 | 0.0000 | 6.93 | 67.14% | yes |
| SwiGLU 20% | 12.9% | 2.78 | 0.0787 | 7.73 | 62.80% | no: INT8 |
| SwiGLU 30% | 19.4% | 2.57 | 0.1161 | 8.17 | 60.70% | no: INT8 |
| SwiGLU 40% | 25.9% | 2.36 | 0.1701 | 8.84 | 58.13% | no: INT8 |
| INT8 PTQ | 32.3% | 2.16 | 0.0030 | 6.94 | 66.99% | yes |
| SwiGLU 50% | 32.4% | 2.16 | 0.2288 | 9.64 | 55.65% | no: INT4 |
| SwiGLU 60% | 38.8% | 1.95 | 0.3040 | 10.84 | 53.60% | no: INT4 |
| SwiGLU 70% | 45.3% | 1.74 | 0.4094 | 12.47 | 50.76% | no: INT4 |
| INT4 PTQ | 47.5% | 1.67 | 0.0896 | 7.42 | 64.95% | no: QAT |
| INT4 QAT 100M | 47.5% | 1.67 | 0.0360 | 7.21 | 65.84% | no: QAT 1B |

- On PPL and macro accuracy the frontier is the same, except that QAT 100M
  has the best PPL at 47.5%.
- SwiGLU reaches the frontier only beyond 47.5%, where no quantized variant
  was tested.
- Longer recovery does not close the gap. SwiGLU 50% after 1B tokens
  (SwiGLU-7) reaches PPL 8.86 and macro 58.49%, still behind INT4 PTQ (7.42,
  64.95%), which needs no training.

## 2. Compute and speed

Linear FLOPs count the weight matrices per generated token (2 FLOPs per
weight: one multiply, one add); attention scores add 196,608 × context FLOPs
for every variant (1.61G at 8,192). Speed comes
from [Speed-1](../experiments/model/baseline/speed-1.md#results-2026-10-15):
one job on one H200, 256 generated tokens, mean of two passes of 10-request
medians. Time to first token (TTFT) repeats within 0.5% between passes,
tokens per second within 6%.

| Variant | Linear FLOPs/token | TTFT, 1 × 7,936 (ms) | TTFT vs dense | Tokens/s, 1 × 2,048 | Tokens/s, 4 × 2,048 | Peak VRAM (GiB) | On the TTFT-KL frontier |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Dense BF16 | 3.42G | 80.2 | 0% | 163.6 | 648.6 | 5.22 | yes |
| INT8 PTQ | 3.42G | 90.5 | +13% | 135.0 | 453.9 | 4.19 | no: dense |
| INT4 PTQ | 3.42G | 82.8 | +3% | 153.5 | 624.5 | 3.70 | no: dense |
| INT4 QAT 1B | 3.42G | 82.7 | +3% | 159.4 | 629.2 | 3.70 | no: dense |
| SwiGLU 20% | 2.98G | 79.0 | -1% | 166.0 | 656.6 | 4.81 | yes |
| SwiGLU 30% | 2.76G | 73.6 | -8% | 161.5 | 640.5 | 4.60 | yes |
| SwiGLU 40% | 2.54G | 70.7 | -12% | 167.4 | 662.9 | 4.39 | yes |
| SwiGLU 50% | 2.32G | 67.1 | -16% | 167.7 | 667.0 | 4.19 | yes |
| SwiGLU 60% | 2.09G | 69.5 | -13% | 167.9 | 666.8 | 3.98 | no: SwiGLU 50% |
| SwiGLU 70% | 1.87G | 65.0 | -19% | 164.4 | 669.4 | 3.78 | yes |
| SwiGLU 75% | 1.76G | 60.8 | -24% | 168.5 | 673.7 | 3.67 | yes |
| SwiGLU 80% | 1.65G | 60.2 | -25% | 164.7 | 673.9 | 3.57 | yes |

- **TTFT:** SwiGLU is faster than dense at every target, 16% at 50% and 25%
  at 80%. Quantization is slower: INT4 by 3%, INT8 by 13%. On the TTFT-KL
  frontier, every quantized variant is beaten by dense itself.
- **Generation speed:** SwiGLU stays within -3% to +4% of dense, inside the
  noise. INT4 is 4-6% slower and INT8 17-30% slower. In this eager PyTorch
  setup, generation is limited by per-token overhead rather than by FLOPs or
  weight bytes (inference; see Speed-1).
- **Peak VRAM:** at equal weight bytes, the two methods use almost the same
  peak memory (INT8 and SwiGLU 50%: 4.19 GiB each).

## 3. Inside SwiGLU: does the local fit matter?

8K KL at 100M recovery tokens. Gap g = slice-only / fitted − 1. The rule calls
a target "matters" only if the monitoring gap changes by less than 1 pp from
50M to 100M.

| Target | 8K KL fitted | 8K KL slice-only | Gap g | Monitoring gap, 50M → 100M | Verdict |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| 20% | 0.0787 | 0.0887 | 12.6% | 22.1% → 16.5% | Inconclusive at 100M |
| 30% | 0.1161 | 0.1288 | 10.9% | 18.9% → 8.3%* | Inconclusive at 100M |
| 40% | 0.1701 | 0.1883 | 10.7% | 14.9% → 11.6% | Inconclusive at 100M |
| 50% | 0.2288 | 0.2527 | 10.4% | 14.9% → 10.4% | Inconclusive at 100M |
| 60% | 0.3040 | 0.3357 | 10.4% | 16.3% → 11.7% | Inconclusive at 100M |
| 70% | 0.4094 | 0.4549 | 11.1% | 17.9% → 11.7% | Inconclusive at 100M |
| 75% | 0.4707 | 0.5815 | 23.5% | 45.6% → 24.1% | Inconclusive at 100M |
| 80% | 0.5433 | 0.6432 | 18.4% | 35.2% → 18.2% | Inconclusive at 100M |

\* The fitted 30% run ends on a single-checkpoint noise bump, so this value is
a few points too low.

- The gap is 5-10 times the 2% "does not matter" bound and above run-to-run
  noise (up to about 3.5%).
- The local fit costs 4-42 GPU-minutes and saves about 51-87M recovery tokens
  (30-54 GPU-minutes).
